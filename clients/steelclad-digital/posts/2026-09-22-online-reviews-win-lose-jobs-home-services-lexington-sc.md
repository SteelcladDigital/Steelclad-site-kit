Here's something worth thinking about.

When someone in Lexington, SC needs a plumber, a roofer, or an HVAC tech, they almost never call the first number they find. They look at [reviews](/services/review-management/) first.

They're looking for reassurance. They want to know that the person showing up at their house is reliable, professional, and actually going to solve the problem. And in a market like Lexington County, where word of mouth has always mattered, online reviews have become the digital version of a neighbor's recommendation.

If your reviews are strong, they convert visitors into callers. If they're weak (or if you barely have any), people move on to the next listing without a second thought.

Here's what most home services businesses in Lexington, SC get wrong about reviews, and what you should be doing instead.

## Your Star Rating Matters More Than You Think

Most people have an informal cutoff. A business with fewer than 4 stars, or fewer than 10 reviews total, gets scrolled past. It's not a conscious decision. It's just how people process trust.

You don't need hundreds of reviews to compete. In most local markets in Lexington County, 20–40 solid reviews with an average of 4.5 stars or better put you in a very strong position relative to most of your competitors.

The businesses that consistently win new customers aren't necessarily the best in town. They're often just the ones with the most visible, consistent, and positive review presence.

## Most Businesses Don't Ask, and That's the Problem

The most common reason home services businesses have thin review profiles is simple: they never asked.

Your satisfied customers aren't going out of their way to leave reviews on their own. They mean to. They even think about it. But life gets in the way, and it never happens.

The fix is to build asking into your process. After every completed job, follow up with a text or email. Keep it short. Say something like: "It was great working with you. If you have a minute, a Google review would mean a lot to us." Include a direct link so it takes them 60 seconds.

That's it. That one change alone will build your review count faster than anything else.

## Bad Reviews Aren't the End of the World, If You Handle Them Right

Every business with a real track record gets a bad review eventually. What separates the professionals from everyone else is how they respond.

Never ignore a negative review. Never get defensive or argue in public. Instead, respond calmly, acknowledge the concern, and offer to make it right. Something simple like: "We're sorry to hear your experience didn't meet your expectations. Please reach out so we can take care of this."

That kind of response tells the next person reading it far more than the negative review did. It says: this business takes feedback seriously and handles problems professionally. That actually builds trust. (More on this in [what happens when a bad review goes unanswered](/blog/b/unanswered-bad-review-impact-lexington-sc/).)

## Your Reputation Doesn't Stop at Google

Google is the most important platform for local search, but it's not the only one. Depending on your trade and your market, reviews on Facebook, Yelp, Houzz, Angi, or Nextdoor can also influence whether someone calls you.

Consistency across platforms (responding to reviews, keeping information accurate, and staying active) signals to both search engines and potential customers that your business is the real deal.

## The Bottom Line

In Lexington County's home services market, your reputation is your most valuable marketing asset. And your online reviews are the public face of that reputation.

The businesses that invest in building a strong, consistent review presence generate better leads, convert more callers, and get more referrals, because trust compounds.
