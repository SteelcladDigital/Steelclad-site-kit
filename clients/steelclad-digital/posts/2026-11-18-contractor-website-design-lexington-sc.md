If you run a home services business in Lexington, SC, you've probably had the calls. Someone offers a "free website." A national company quotes a monthly fee that never ends. A friend's nephew says he can knock one out in a weekend. They all promise a site that looks great.

Looking great isn't the job. A contractor website has one job: turn the people who find you on Google into phone calls and booked work. This guide covers what that takes, what it should cost, and the questions to ask any web designer in Lexington before you sign anything.

## What a Contractor Website Actually Needs to Do

Most homeowners find a contractor the same way: a Google search on their phone, a quick look at the Google Business Profile, then a tap through to the website to decide whether to call. Your site gets a few seconds to pass that test.

That means a good contractor website needs to:

- **Say what you do and where, right away.** "Roof Repair and Replacement in Lexington, SC" beats "Welcome to Our Website" every time.
- **Make calling effortless.** A tap-to-call phone number at the top of every page, and a short form for people who'd rather not call.
- **Load fast on a phone.** Most of your visitors are on mobile, often on a cell connection in their driveway.
- **Have a page for each service and the towns you serve.** One "Services" page can't rank for "AC repair Irmo SC" and "water heater replacement West Columbia" at the same time.
- **Show real proof.** Your own job photos, your real reviews, how long you've been working in Lexington County.

## Design and SEO Aren't Separate Projects

A lot of businesses pay one company to build a website and later pay someone else to "do the SEO." That usually means paying twice, because the second company has to fix what the first one built.

Ranking in Lexington starts with how the site is put together:

- **One clear topic per page,** with a title and heading that match what people search
- **Clean page addresses** like "/services/roof-repair/" instead of "/page-id-4471"
- **Structured data** that tells Google your business name, phone, hours, service area and services
- **Pages written for the towns you serve,** with real local detail rather than the same paragraph with the city name swapped

Build those in from day one and your site starts earning visibility instead of needing a rescue later. (More on how this works in [local SEO for home services businesses](/services/local-seo/).)

## What Should a Contractor Website Cost in Lexington, SC?

You'll see a huge range, and the price on its own doesn't tell you much. What matters is what you get and what you keep. The common options:

- **Do-it-yourself builders.** Cheap to start and fine for a placeholder, but you're the designer, the copywriter and the SEO, and most look and rank like it.
- **"Free" or cheap websites bundled with a marketing platform or directory.** Watch who owns the site. If you stop paying, you often lose the website, the content and sometimes the phone number.
- **One-time builds from a freelancer or design shop.** Can be great. Ask what happens after launch: who updates it, who hosts it, who fixes it when something breaks.
- **A monthly plan that includes the website.** One predictable price that covers the build, hosting, updates and ongoing work to get you found.

## 7 Questions to Ask Any Web Designer in Lexington

Before you hire anyone, us included, get straight answers to these:

1. **Who owns the website, the domain and the content?** The answer should be "you." Get it in writing.
2. **What happens if I cancel?** Is there an exit fee? Do you keep the site, or does it disappear?
3. **Is there a long-term contract?** If they need a 12-month lock-in to keep you, ask why.
4. **How fast will it load on a phone?** Ask to see a speed test on a site they've built. You can run any site through Google's free PageSpeed Insights yourself.
5. **Will I get a page for each service and each town I serve?** Or one generic page for everything?
6. **Where do my leads go, and how will I know where they came from?** You should get every form submission instantly and see which page, search or ad brought it in.
7. **Who will I actually talk to?** The person who sold you the site, or a rotating support queue?

If a designer gets vague on ownership, cancellation or who you'll work with, that tells you what you need to know.

## Red Flags to Watch For

- **A "free" website with a long contract attached.** The website isn't free; it's the hook.
- **Guaranteed first-page rankings.** Nobody controls Google. Anyone promising a specific position is guessing or not telling you the whole story.
- **No access to your own accounts.** You should be an owner on your domain, Google Business Profile, analytics and ad accounts, not a guest.
- **A pretty template with your logo dropped in.** If it looks exactly like a dozen other contractor sites, it'll perform like them too.
