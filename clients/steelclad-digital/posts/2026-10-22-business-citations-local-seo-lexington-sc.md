Ask most home services business owners in Lexington, SC what "citations" are, and you'll get a blank stare. That's understandable. It's not a term that shows up in everyday conversation. But citations are quietly one of the most important factors in whether your business ranks well in local search.

This post explains what citations are, why they matter, and what to do about them if yours are a mess.

## What Is a Citation?

A citation is any online mention of your business's name, address, and phone number. Together, these three pieces of information are referred to as NAP: Name, Address, Phone.

Citations show up in places like:

- Google Business Profile
- Yelp
- Angi (formerly Angie's List)
- HomeAdvisor
- Facebook Business Page
- Better Business Bureau
- Houzz
- Thumbtack
- Nextdoor
- Bing Places
- Apple Maps
- Industry-specific directories

Every time your business information appears in one of these places, that's a citation. And Google collects them.

## Why Google Cares About Citations

Google's entire goal is to show people the most accurate, trustworthy, and relevant results for their search. When someone in Lexington, SC searches "electrician near me," Google wants to be confident it's showing a real, established, local business, not a spam listing or a fly-by-night operation.

Citations help [Google verify your business](/services/google-business-profile/). If your name, address, and phone number appear consistently across dozens of reputable online directories, that's a strong trust signal. It says: this business is real, it's where it says it is, and it's been around long enough to be listed across the web.

More citations from more reputable sources = more trust = higher rankings.

## The Problem Most Local Businesses Have: Inconsistency

Here's where things go wrong. Most home services businesses in Lexington, SC have some citations, but they're inconsistent.

Maybe you moved locations two years ago, and your old address still shows up on three directories. Maybe your business name is "Johnson Plumbing" on Google but "Johnson Plumbing & Drain Services LLC" on Yelp. Maybe you changed your phone number and the old one is still live on six sites.

To a human, these look like minor variations. To Google's algorithm, they're red flags. Inconsistent NAP data creates doubt. And when Google has doubt about your business information, it loses confidence, which means lower rankings.

This is one of the most common silent [SEO](/services/local-seo/) problems for home services businesses. It doesn't create an obvious error. Nothing breaks. You just gradually fall behind competitors who have cleaner citation profiles.

## How Many Citations Do You Need?

There's no magic number, but a solid baseline for a home services business in a market like Lexington, SC is 40–60 consistent, accurate citations across relevant directories and platforms.

***Quality matters more than quantity.*** A citation on a high-authority, well-known site like Yelp or the Better Business Bureau carries more weight than a listing on a random directory no one has heard of. Focus on:

- **General local directories:** Google, Yelp, Bing Places, Apple Maps, Facebook
- **Home services-specific directories:** Angi, HomeAdvisor, Houzz, Thumbtack, Porch
- **Regional directories:** South Carolina Chamber of Commerce, Lexington County business listings, local news sites
- **Industry association listings:** PHCC for plumbers, NARI for remodelers, etc.

## What Good Citation Management Looks Like

It's not a one-time task. Citation management is ongoing because directories can change, aggregate your data incorrectly, or allow others to suggest edits to your listing.

Good citation management includes:

- **Audit first:** find every place your business is listed and identify any inaccuracies
- **Clean up inconsistencies:** correct your NAP across every listing until it's identical
- **Build new citations:** submit to directories where you aren't listed yet
- **Monitor for changes:** check periodically to make sure nothing has drifted

This process isn't glamorous, but it's foundational. It's the kind of thing that doesn't produce immediate visible results but quietly supports everything else you're doing for local SEO.

## A Real-World Example

Imagine two HVAC companies in Lexington, SC. Both do excellent work. Both have decent websites and a reasonable number of Google reviews.

Company A has 55 consistent citations. Their NAP is identical across every directory. They're listed on Angi, Yelp, HomeAdvisor, BBB, Houzz, and all the major platforms.

Company B has 20 listings, several with an old address and two different phone numbers.

Everything else being equal, Company A is going to outrank Company B in local search. Not because they're better at their trade. Because Google trusts them more.

## The Bottom Line

Citations aren't flashy. They don't make for exciting marketing conversations. But they are a legitimate ranking factor, and cleaning them up is one of the most reliable things you can do to improve your local search visibility in Lexington, SC.

If you're not sure what your citation profile looks like, that's worth finding out before you spend another dollar on advertising.
