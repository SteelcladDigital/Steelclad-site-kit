September in Lexington is a funny month for pool owners.

The summer chaos has slowed down. School is back in session. The kids aren't piling into the pool every afternoon. And somewhere in the back of your mind, you're starting to wonder: ***is it time to close the pool?***

Here's the short answer: probably not yet.

Closing too early is one of the most common — and most costly — mistakes Lexington homeowners make when it comes to fall pool care. And if you close at the wrong time, you can actually create more problems than you're trying to avoid.

Here's what you actually need to know about pool closings in the Midlands.

## Why September Is Too Early to Close Your Pool in Lexington

South Carolina doesn't follow the same seasonal script as the rest of the country. Up north, Labor Day is the end of pool season. Here in the Midlands, water temperatures routinely hold in the upper 70s through September — and often into October.

That matters because of one thing: **algae**.

Algae grows aggressively in warm water. If you seal your pool under a winter cover while the water is still 78°F, you're essentially creating a perfect environment for algae to take hold all winter long. By the time you pull that cover off in spring, you could be staring at a [green, swamp-like mess](/services/green-to-clean-pool-recovery/) that requires a full green-to-clean treatment before anyone swims.

**The right time to close your pool is when water temperature consistently holds below 60°F.** In Lexington, that's typically mid-to-late November — sometimes December. Not September. Not October.

## So What Should You Be Doing With Your Pool in September?

September is a transition month — and if you use it right, you set yourself up for a smooth closing *and* an easy spring opening.

Here's what smart pool owners are doing in September in the Midlands:

### 1. Keep Testing and Balancing Chemistry

Don't assume that because bather load is down, chemistry is on autopilot. September still brings warm days, afternoon rain, and wind-blown debris — all of which affect your water balance.

Test your water at least weekly. At minimum, check:

- **Free chlorine:** 1–3 ppm
- **pH:** 7.4–7.6
- **Total alkalinity:** 80–120 ppm
- **Cyanuric acid (stabilizer):** 30–50 ppm

One thing to start doing in September: let your cyanuric acid (stabilizer) levels come down a bit if they've crept high during the summer. High CYA makes chlorine less effective — a problem that follows you into the closing chemistry if you ignore it.

### 2. Stay on Top of Debris

September brings falling leaves, pine needles, and wind-blown debris — especially as the trees start to change. That organic matter is a phosphate factory. Phosphates feed algae. Let debris sit in your pool, and you're stocking the algae pantry for free.

Skim the surface regularly, empty your skimmer baskets every few days, and brush the walls and floor at least once a week. This takes 15 minutes, but it makes a real difference in water clarity and chemistry stability heading into fall.

### 3. Dial Back the Pump — But Not Too Much

You don't need to run your pump 10–12 hours a day in September the way you did in July. As water temperatures drop, algae risk decreases, and filtration needs reduce. You can typically pull back to 6–8 hours per day once water temps are consistently below 75°F.

But don't get too aggressive. Pool owners who cut pump run time too early in the fall often end up with circulation problems and cloudy water right when they're trying to close. Keep your filtration consistent until you're within a few weeks of actual closing.

### 4. Inspect Your Equipment While It's Still Easy

Fall is the ideal time to catch small equipment issues before they become expensive winter emergencies. While the pump, heater, and filter are still running, look for:

- Unusual noises from the pump motor
- Visible leaks around fittings, valves, or the pump lid
- Filter pressure that's running abnormally high (clogged filter)
- Heater that's slow to heat or not reaching target temperature

A minor pump issue caught in September is a simple fix. That same issue ignored until spring — when it's seized from a winter freeze — is a $600–$1,200 repair and a pool that's out of commission at the start of swim season.

### 5. Start Planning Your Closing Date

Even if you're not closing yet, September is the right time to plan. A few things to think about:

- **Schedule your closing service now.** Pool service companies fill up fast in October and November. Don't wait until you're ready to close and then find out there's a two-week wait.
- **Decide on a cover.** A solid cover keeps debris out but requires monitoring for water accumulation. A mesh safety cover lets water through but blocks most debris. Talk to your pool professional about what makes sense for your setup.
- **Think about a closing chemical kit.** Your closing chemistry should include a quality algaecide and a proper shock treatment timed correctly — not just whatever's left over in the garage from last season.

## The September Mindset: Stay Consistent, Plan Ahead

Most pool problems don't happen because people did something wrong. They happen because people stopped paying attention too early.

September is the month when it's tempting to mentally check out — the season feels over even if the water's still warm. The pools we see come out of winter in the worst shape are almost always the ones where the owner started slacking in September and closed too early in October.

The pools that open clean every spring? Those owners stayed consistent through the transition months and closed at the right time, with the right chemistry.
