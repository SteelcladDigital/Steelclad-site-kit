The short answer? More often than most people think.

South Carolina's heat, humidity, and long swim season create conditions that demand consistent, routine pool care. Skip a week, and your water chemistry can spiral fast. ## Weekly Pool Service: The Non-Negotiable Baseline

For residential pools in the Lexington area, **weekly service is the minimum standard** — not a luxury. South Carolina summers are hot and long, which means algae growth, chemical imbalance, and debris buildup happen fast.

A proper weekly service should include:

- **Water chemistry testing and balancing** — pH, free chlorine, total alkalinity, and cyanuric acid levels need to be in range every single week. When they drift, you get cloudy water, eye irritation, and equipment damage.
- **Skimming the surface** — Leaves, bugs, pollen, and debris accumulate quickly, especially during spring and fall. Letting it pile up clogs your skimmer basket and strains your pump.
- **Brushing walls and steps** — Algae clings to pool surfaces before it ever turns the water green. Regular brushing keeps it from taking hold.
- **Vacuuming the pool floor** — Settled debris and fine particles belong in the filter, not sitting on your liner or plaster.
- **Equipment check** — A quick look at your pump, filter, and skimmer basket catches small problems before they become expensive ones.

## Monthly Pool Maintenance: Going Deeper

Beyond the weekly basics, there are a handful of tasks that need attention every four to six weeks:

**Filter cleaning (every 4–6 weeks).** Your filter is your pool's immune system. Cartridge filters need to be removed and rinsed regularly. DE and sand filters need backwashing. A clogged or dirty filter can't do its job — and when it fails, your water clarity suffers immediately.

**Full equipment inspection (monthly).** Pump pressure, return jets, skimmer function, heater performance, and automation settings should all be checked on a monthly basis. Catching a worn seal, a pressure spike, or a failing capacitor early saves you from a $1,000+ emergency later. Our [pump and filter service](/services/pool-pump-filter-repair/) team handles exactly this.

**Waterline tile and surface brushing.** The waterline is where calcium scale, sunscreen, and organic buildup concentrate. Monthly attention keeps it from hardening into a problem that requires acid washing to fix.

## Seasonal Pool Care in South Carolina

Here's something worth knowing about pool service in our part of the state: **South Carolina doesn't really have a true off-season.** While homeowners in colder climates close their pools in October and forget about them until May, most Lexington-area pools are swim-ready eight or nine months out of the year — and need basic monitoring year-round.

**Spring Opening (March–April).** This is when your pool wakes up after winter. It needs a thorough chemical balance, equipment startup check, and often a shock treatment to clear any early algae growth. Skipping this or rushing it is the #1 reason pools turn green by May.

**Summer Peak (May–September).** This is your heaviest maintenance window. High heat, heavy bather load, UV intensity, and South Carolina humidity all work against water chemistry. Weekly service isn't optional during these months — it's essential. Pools left unserviced for even 10–14 days in July can turn green within 48 hours.

**Fall Transition (October–November).** As temperatures drop, algae growth slows — but your pool still needs attention. Falling leaves create a debris load that can overwhelm a skimmer basket fast. Chemical needs shift as water temperatures drop.

**Winter Monitoring (December–February).** Even in "off season," South Carolina pools benefit from bi-weekly check-ins. Freezing temps are rare but not impossible, and stagnant water with no circulation or chemical maintenance can develop problems that are costly to fix come spring.

## What Happens When You Skip Service?

This is where homeowners usually learn the hard way. Here's what inconsistent pool maintenance actually costs:

- **Green water** — Algae blooms can turn a pool green in 48–72 hours during peak summer. Our [Green to Clean recovery service](/services/green-to-clean-pool-recovery/) can fix it, but it's always better and cheaper to prevent it.
- **Cloudy water** — Usually a chemistry imbalance or filtration failure. It can require multiple treatments and a few days to clear.
- **Scaling and staining** — Calcium buildup and metal staining are difficult to reverse once they set. They're almost entirely preventable with consistent chemistry management.
- **Equipment failure** — Running a pump with a clogged filter, low water level, or ignored warning signs leads to burned-out motors and cracked pump housing. These repairs run hundreds to thousands of dollars.

Consistent weekly service — typically far less expensive than one repair call — prevents all of it.

## Do You Really Need a Professional, or Can You DIY?

You can absolutely manage your own pool — if you're willing to invest the time to learn water chemistry, stay consistent every single week regardless of weather or schedule, and catch equipment issues early. Some homeowners do it well.

But most don't. And that's not a knock on anyone — it's just that pool chemistry is more nuanced than dropping in a chlorine tablet and calling it a day. The pH, alkalinity, cyanuric acid, calcium hardness, and sanitizer levels all interact with each other. Getting one wrong throws off the others.
